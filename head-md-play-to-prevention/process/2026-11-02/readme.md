# Let It Slide

## Pitch
The game explores how cyberbullying spreads when nobody steps in, and turns the 5Ds of bystander intervention into a toolkit learned through play. It unfolds as a series of connected cases inside one online community, and each case adds a page to the player's own field kit.

## The Game

### Starting Point
- Online harassment toolkits already hold the right information, but they ask to be read, and few people will read them. The game starts from the idea that this knowledge sticks better when it is used.
- Each harmful post left unanswered makes the next one seem more acceptable, until bullying becomes normal and anyone can be the next target.

### Mechanics
- The player is an ordinary member of the community and cannot delete posts or ban anyone.
- Each case has two steps. The player first identifies what is happening, such as a rumour, a pile-on, an impersonation or an exclusion. They then respond with one or several of the 5Ds: Direct, Distract, Delegate, Delay and Document.
- The community keeps moving while the player decides. When a case is ignored, the snowball grows: more accounts join in, silent members copy what they see, and others quietly leave.
- Intervening reverses the effect. When one person acts, others who stayed silent begin to show support.
- Each resolved case unlocks a short field kit page explaining what to look for, what to say and what to do. The player can keep using this kit after the game.

### Narrative
- The story follows one community over several weeks, told only through its feed, group chats and private messages.
- The cases are connected, so a target in one case may be a bystander or an aggressor in the next. There is no single villain to catch.
- In the final chapter, the player writes the community's rules and sees whether they hold.

## Conclusion
At what point does a joke become the norm? If nobody steps in, who is next? To denounce something, we first need to be able to identify it. Players leave having seen how bullying spreads, and with a toolkit they built themselves.
